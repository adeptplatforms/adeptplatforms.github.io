What is due when

The Code of Practice on the provision of information for postgraduate medical training (England, 2017) sets three notice points before a doctor starts a placement:

  • 12 weeks: the deanery (NHS England's local office) tells the doctor and the employer about the placement: the post and its level, the employer, the site, the start date and how long it lasts.
  • 8 weeks: the employer sends the post's details, including hours and on-call pattern, pay, leave and supervisors, and the generic work schedule. Under the 2016 terms and conditions the 8-week work schedule is contractual.
  • 6 weeks: the employer sends the duty roster, which is the doctor's actual rota slot.

The full walk-through, including who is responsible and what to do when a deadline is missed, is in our guide to Code of Practice notice periods.

When do rotations change?

It depends on the specialty and the region. Most specialty training in England starts on the first Wednesday of August, and some programmes, including anaesthetics, also rotate in February. Others rotate at different points in the year, and Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland set their own dates. Choose the months your programme uses above.

Why anaesthetics training ends on a Tuesday

A placement runs up to the day before the next changeover, so a doctor finishing at a Wednesday changeover finishes on the Tuesday. The RCoA's CCT date calculator moves an anaesthetics completion date within six days of the February or August changeover to that Tuesday. Work out a date with the free CCT date calculator.
