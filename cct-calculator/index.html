An estimate, not a confirmed date. Your deanery confirms a CCT date, usually at ARCP, and decides how sickness, statutory leave and time out count. Under the Gold Guide, 14 or more days of absence in a year (not counting study or annual leave) triggers a review of the date at ARCP. This tool is independent of the colleges named; it reproduces the counting in their published spreadsheets.

How each method counts

Every college agrees on the principle: training time is pro rata, so 80% for a year counts as 0.8 of a year, and time out of training does not count. They differ in the arithmetic, which is why the same pattern can give dates a few days apart.

For intensive care medicine, physician specialties, obstetrics and gynaecology, surgery, psychiatry and general practice, the college publishes no counting formula: completion dates are set by the deanery or the college's training body. The calculator uses simple pro rata for these and says so.

Emergency medicine changed how it defines full time in August 2025: from ST3, the training percentage is the average hours actually worked against a 40-hour week, in 10% bands, not the contracted WTE.

Frequently asked

Does annual leave count as training?

Yes. Annual and study leave are part of training time. Annual leave that builds up during maternity or parental leave also counts in the RCoA's calculator.

Why is my date a Tuesday?

In the RCoA method, a date within six days of the changeover moves to the Tuesday before the first Wednesday of February or August, so training ends the day before the next rotation starts.

Can I share the result?

Yes. "Copy a link to this" puts the dates and pattern in the link itself, after the # sign, which browsers never send to a server. Anyone with the link sees the same calculation.

Is this the official calculator?

No. It reproduces the counting in the colleges' published spreadsheets, and the answer is an estimate. Your Training Programme Director and deanery confirm your date.
